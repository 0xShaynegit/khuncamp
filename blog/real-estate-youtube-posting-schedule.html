How Often Should Real Estate Agents Post on YouTube

The most common YouTube question agents ask after starting a channel: how often do I need to post? The honest answer is less about frequency and more about sustainability — and what you do with the library you're building.

Shawn ArringtonAug 20265 min read

YouTube does reward consistency. But "consistency" is often misread as volume. The agents who burn out at three months aren't failing because they posted too infrequently — they're failing because they committed to a pace they couldn't maintain and quit when the schedule broke.

The minimum viable posting frequency

One video per week is the benchmark most YouTube coaches cite. For agents with a production system in place, it's achievable. For an agent filming, editing, and uploading alone while also running a real estate business, it's usually not realistic for long.

The actual minimum that still produces results: one video per week for the first 12 weeks. This is the library-building phase. You're not going viral, you're not building subscribers — you're getting enough indexed content on the channel for the algorithm to understand what you're about and start surfacing it to the right viewers.

After that initial 12-week push, one video every 1–2 weeks is sustainable for most agents and still produces compounding growth over 12–18 months.

Why the first 12 weeks matter most

New channels with thin libraries don't rank well in search because YouTube hasn't seen enough content to classify the channel as a topical authority. Getting to 12–15 videos quickly establishes that pattern. It's also when you improve fastest — your 12th video will be noticeably better than your first, and getting there faster means you're improving in real time rather than strung across months.

The compounding math also favors an early push. A video you publish in month one has 12 more months to accumulate views and subscribers than a video you publish in month 12. Front-loading the library accelerates everything that comes after.

Quality versus frequency: the real tradeoff

The tradeoff between quality and frequency is real, but it's not binary. A well-structured 10-minute video with decent audio, clear answers, and a searchable title will outperform a polished but poorly-targeted video almost every time. You don't need to choose between posting often and posting well — you need to get efficient enough to do both.

What actually slows most agents down isn't filming or scripting — it's editing. If editing is the bottleneck, that's the lever to pull: batch film multiple videos in one day, outsource editing, or simplify the production style so there's less to cut. One talking-head video filmed well requires minimal post-production.

What to do when you fall off schedule

Most channels miss a week at some point. The temptation after missing one is to either double up the next week (unsustainable) or give up entirely (the more common outcome). Neither is right.

Missing a week doesn't reset your channel. YouTube's search traffic doesn't care that you missed a week — your indexed videos keep generating views and subscribers whether you posted this week or not. The right move is to return to the schedule and keep going. The agents who stay in the game long enough almost always win.

The posting schedule that actually works

Pick a day and time and stick to it. Tuesday through Thursday mornings tend to perform well for real estate content — viewers researching a move are often active early in the week. But the specific day matters far less than the pattern. Set a publish day, schedule videos in advance so the habit is locked in, and treat it like a client appointment: it goes on the calendar and it happens.

A realistic posting plan for an agent running a business: batch film on one day per month, produce 4 videos, schedule one per week. That's it. One production day a month is manageable for almost any schedule — and it removes the weekly decision-fatigue that causes most agents to fall off.
