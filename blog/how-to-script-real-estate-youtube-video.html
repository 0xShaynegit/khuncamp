How to Script a Real Estate YouTube Video

Over-scripted videos sound robotic. Fully unscripted videos ramble and lose viewers in the first minute. Here's the middle approach that keeps you natural on camera while making sure the video actually lands.

Shawn ArringtonAug 20265 min read

The goal of a script isn't to read from it word-for-word. It's to know exactly where the video is going before you press record — so you don't waste takes, don't lose your thread, and don't leave the viewer wondering why they're still watching.

Most real estate agents who struggle on camera are struggling with structure, not confidence. Once the structure is clear, the confidence follows.

The three-part structure that works

Every real estate YouTube video — regardless of topic — can follow this structure:

  • Hook (first 30 seconds) — tell viewers exactly what they're going to get and why it matters to them. Not a long intro, not your name and brokerage, not "welcome back." Start with the payoff: "In this video I'm going to show you the three neighborhoods in [city] under $400k that most buyers never look at." That's a hook. It answers "why should I keep watching" in the first sentence.
  • Body — deliver what you promised. Keep it organized. If you're covering three things, say "the first thing is…" and "the second thing is…" Explicit signposting keeps viewers oriented and reduces drop-off. Each point should be tight — one idea, one or two sentences of explanation, move on.
  • CTA (last 60 seconds) — one clear ask. Book a call, download the guide, watch the next video. Not three things — one. Viewers who made it to the end are your warmest audience. Don't waste that with a vague "hope this was helpful."

What to actually write in the script

Don't write full sentences to read aloud. Write bullet points — the key idea you want to hit in each section. Then talk through it naturally on camera. This keeps you sounding like yourself instead of someone reading a document.

For the hook, write it out word for word. The first 30 seconds are too important to improvise. Know exactly what you're going to say before you hit record, deliver it clean, then move to bullets for the rest.

How long should a real estate YouTube video be

Long enough to fully answer the question, short enough to not waste the viewer's time. For most real estate topics — neighborhood guides, buyer tips, market updates — 8 to 12 minutes is the sweet spot. It's long enough for YouTube to treat the video as substantive, short enough that viewers can finish it in a sitting.

Don't pad for length. Don't cut for the sake of keeping it short. The right length is whatever it takes to fully deliver what you promised in the hook.

The one thing that kills watch time

A slow intro. Every second you spend saying "hey guys, welcome back, don't forget to like and subscribe, today we're going to be talking about…" is a second the viewer uses to click away. YouTube measures how long people watch from the very first frame. A 30-second intro that doesn't deliver value is 30 seconds of damage to your retention metrics.

Start with the hook. Save the channel plug for the end — if they watched the whole thing, they already know they should subscribe.
